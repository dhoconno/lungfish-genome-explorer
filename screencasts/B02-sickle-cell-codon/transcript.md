# Find the sickle cell codon

Filmed with Lungfish 2026.9.72. The narration is a synthetic voice.

This video uses the Genes and Sequences demo project. Choose Help, then Demo Projects, and click Download and Open beside it. It holds NG_000007.3, the NCBI reference record for the human beta-globin gene cluster. The video Your first project shows each step.

Click NG_000007.3 under Reference Sequences, then click Focus to show it in the viewer. It holds HBB, the gene for beta-globin, one of the protein chains of hemoglobin, which carries oxygen in red blood cells. Annotated features, such as genes and coding sequences, show as colored bars.

A codon is three bases in a gene's coding sequence that specify one amino acid. Choose Sequence, then Go to Location, type the record name and the codon's first and last bases, 70,613 to 70,615, and click Go. The viewer zooms to three bases, GAG.

The Translation row below the bases shows the amino acid each codon specifies, as a one-letter code. GAG specifies glutamate, also called glutamic acid, written E.

In sickle hemoglobin, HbS, the middle A of this codon is a T. GTG specifies valine, so amino acid 6 of beta-globin becomes valine instead of glutamate. When oxygen is low, HbS forms long fibers that bend red blood cells into a sickle shape. Sickle cell disease needs HbS from both parents.

An annotation is your own labeled mark on a stretch of sequence. Drag across the three bases, choose Sequence, then Add Annotation, type a name, and click Add.

LGE saves your annotation in the project. Go back to the codon and it shows on the Manual Annotations track, a row of the viewer under the bases.
