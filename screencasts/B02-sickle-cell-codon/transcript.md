# Find the sickle cell codon

Filmed with Lungfish 2026.9.72. The narration is a synthetic voice.

This video uses the Genes and Sequences demo project. Choose Help, then Demo Projects, and click Download and Open beside it. It holds NG_000007.3, the human beta globin record from NCBI GenBank. The video Your first project shows each step.

Click NG_000007.3 under Reference Sequences, then Focus. It holds the beta globin gene, which makes part of hemoglobin, the protein that carries oxygen in blood. Genes show as colored bars called features.

A codon is three bases that code for one amino acid, a building block of protein. Choose Sequence, then Go to Location, type the position of the codon that changes in sickle cell disease, and click Go. The viewer zooms to three bases, GAG.

The red row below the bases shows the amino acid each codon codes for. GAG codes for glutamic acid, written E.

In sickle cell disease the middle A is a T. The codon becomes GTG, which codes for valine instead. The changed hemoglobin sticks together and bends red blood cells into a sickle shape.

To mark the codon, drag across the three bases, choose Sequence, then Add Annotation, type a name, and click Add.

LGE saves your annotation in the project. Go back to the codon and it appears on its own track, under the bases.
