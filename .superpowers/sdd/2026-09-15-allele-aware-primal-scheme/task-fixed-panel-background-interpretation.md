## What this establishes—and what it does not

This is strong controlled evidence that the current cross-target background screen can reject much of this useful A1 panel even with no competition from other-target primers. It isolates a real feasibility restriction independently of discovery time, optimizer scheduling, and new primer-pool interactions. It makes cross-target specificity a priority explanation to examine in a completed combined run.

It does **not** establish that the same gate dominates the optimized full11 loss: a combined search could choose different A1 variants, different configurations or pools; discovery availability and search limits may also matter. Nor is80/90 a probability of experimental failure. Newly failed unary configurations cannot be rescued merely by moving the unchanged configuration to another pool; pair failures may be avoided by pool separation or changing either member, but those alternatives have not been tested. Do not subtract all implicated configurations and present the remainder as a necessary coverage ceiling.

The existing intended/secondary certificates are deliberately target-owned. An A1 configuration's selected anchors cannot authorize an A2 product just because its sequence is similar or both MSAs were supplied as desired design targets. Separate MSAs have separate alignment coordinate systems. A1 positionX is not inherently an intended A2 positionX. The full11 input list says which targets the design should cover, not that every inward product anywhere in any one of those targets is acceptable.

Conversely, an assay deliberately designed to coamplify specified homologous target regions could regard some cross-locus products as useful. That is an assay contract requiring explicit target/region/primer-role declarations and product evidence. It cannot be inferred from these screening witnesses or from the supplied-target membership alone. The current panel objective does not credit those A2/A4/B/E products; removing their rejection would therefore not by itself constitute demonstrated useful partial coverage of those targets.

## Optional policy assessment

No new conservative production exemption is justified by the current aggregate counts alone. In particular reject “any product in a supplied target is intended,” “all A1/A2/A4 products are expected,” and reuse of a same-target certificate after silently dropping its target identity check. Those can permit shifted, reversed, long, mismatched or unintended extra products, and can combine ends from different configurations.

A possible future narrow policy would require a **predeclared, paired intended-site role on each additional target**, independent of the observed rejection list. Each certificate would bind the exact selected physical forward/reverse species, full target/row occurrence, both endpoints, orientation, desired product geometry/range, complete observed footprints, and unchanged terminal predicates. The most conservative first candidate would require full exact binding on both ends. For a secondary product it would additionally require the complete four-site, ordered/disjoint, same-row, pair-local geometry on that additional target—not merely two convenient external hits. Certificates would never cover uncertain or undeclared/shifted occurrences, would not exempt unrelated products from the same primers, and would need a separate policy/cache/witness identity plus authoritative-row fresh audit.

Where do the declared additional-target sites come from? A reviewed cross-target coordinate/region map or explicit target-specific site/family roles could supply them. The unfinished full11 catalog cannot be assumed to have those roles, and discovery of a matching sequence alone is not a declaration of intended biology. Requiring another selected target-specific configuration at those sites is a stronger ownership model, but that evaluates a genuine combined selection, not this A1-only experiment. Either approach needs design review and targeted falsification before any code. Simply projecting A1 alignment anchors into another MSA is invalid.

Coverage credit must remain separate. A screening exemption can conservatively retain zero added coverage, as prior policies do; scientifically counting useful multi-target products would require an explicitly reviewed extension of configuration ownership and exact same-row coverage accounting. Do not introduce that objective change as an incidental specificity fix.
