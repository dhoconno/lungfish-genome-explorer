# Specificity compatibility design review

Decision: amend the proposed profile before implementation. The current intended-pair-only exemption is incompatible with ordinary same-row tiling under the stated distance bound. This finding follows from interval geometry; it does not depend on observed coverage, parameter tuning, or assumptions about laboratory amplification efficiency. Scope: supplied human HLA alignments and synthetic fixtures.

## Defect and its limits

Take perfectly specific intended products A=[0,200) and B=[300,500) on a 1,000-base row. In one pool, A's forward primer and B's reverse primer face inward and define [0,500). Both bind their declared intended sites. Nevertheless, the proposed rule rejects that product because its endpoints belong to different pairs and its 500-base span is below 2,000. Every pair of ordered disjoint products on that row has the same problem.

When all selected candidates have intended support on a common row, the rule therefore allows at most one candidate per pool per locus. With two pools and a 280-base maximum, full-span coverage of a 1,000-base locus cannot exceed 560 bases. Disjoint allele-support sets can change that bound, so this is not a universal theorem for every catalogue; it is already a decisive counterexample for the normal common-row case. Correcting this defect does not guarantee 90% coverage or laboratory performance.

The longer cross-combination is still a possible secondary product. The profile must disclose that it tolerates this specific category. It must not call it absent, claim every product is a declared pair, or exclude its endpoints from subsequent offsite checks.

## Pure, row-aware checker contract

Use a separately identified semantic revision, preferably `panel-v2` if any artifact has already named the old behavior `panel-v1`. An unreleased profile can retain its public name only if its exact specificity revision is separately recorded. Freeze the following rules before benchmarks. Include the revision, coordinate/product convention, k-mer length, mismatch rule, ambiguity policy and distance bound in cache keys and provenance.

A hit retains target occurrence ID, row occurrence ID, actual 5'-3' oligo identity, orientation, terminal-match interval and mismatch/support classification. Never join hits across different rows, even within the same MSA. Never discard cross-MSA hits. Duplicate identical MSAs remain distinct target occurrences and may conservatively produce offsite conflicts; this change neither merges them nor introduces multi-target coverage.

Compute in ungapped row coordinates using alignment anchors. For an actual oligo of length L, a plus-oriented terminal-k match [u,u+k) projects the full footprint [u+k-L,u+k); a minus-oriented match [v,v+k) projects [v,v+L). The latter is the reverse complement of the actual oligo's 3' terminal k-mer. If the plus footprint is [a,b) and minus footprint [c,d), facing products require b <= c and have predicted full product interval [a,d), length d-a. Adjacent 3' ends are included; crossed 3' ends are not genomic facing products. Oligo interactions remain subject to the independent chemistry kernel. State this convention explicitly rather than using the legacy difference between k-mer start positions.

Enumerate possible products with 0 < length <= D, where D is the resolved specificity bound. The inequality is inclusive and coverage mode requires D > 0. A terminal-only hit predicts a possible binding/product event; it does not establish full-primer binding. A projected footprint extending into unavailable sequence is uncertain, not an intended exemption. Preserve the existing exact/single-mismatch detection policy; do not weaken it to repair the tiling defect.

For each evaluated candidate, give hits ownership labels tied to that candidate's declared primer occurrence. `intended(C, role, oligo, hit, row)` requires the same target occurrence as C, the correct role/orientation, the exact mapped anchor, and confirmed full-oligo support for that concrete member at that anchor on this row. Merely matching its terminal k-mer, or being intended for a different candidate, is insufficient. The candidate's row must also have confirmed joint intended forward/reverse support. Unknown/uncertain support alone cannot grant an exemption; preserve those diagnostics.

## Exact product exemptions

Classify every eligible product in this order:

1. **Declared-pair product:** exempt when the physical product exactly matches a declared-pair signature of an evaluated candidate on that row, with confirmed support for both concrete endpoint oligos. For I(A), only A's signatures are available. For P(A,B), either A's or B's signatures suffice, regardless of which candidate occurrence supplied an identical oligo during enumeration. A signature contains target occurrence, row occurrence, both actual oligo sequences, both orientations and exact anchored footprints; sharing sequence alone does not establish a signature match.
2. **Ordered disjoint intended-site cross-combination:** when evaluating candidates A and B, exempt a product only if both belong to the same target occurrence, both have confirmed joint support on this exact row, their actual row-level intended full-product intervals are disjoint and ordered, and its endpoints are the intended forward of the left candidate and intended reverse of the right candidate. Both concrete endpoint oligos must satisfy `intended`. The longer product spans the outer boundaries of the two declared products. Record it as an allowed secondary product when within D.
3. **All other eligible products:** conflict. This includes any off-anchor or wrong-orientation endpoint, any endpoint on another target occurrence, products from additional hits within a single candidate, and cross-products between overlapping or nested intended intervals. Do not exempt a product merely because both oligo sequences occur somewhere among intended primers.

For example, overlapping A=[0,200), B=[150,350), with 20-base endpoint primers, has a short inward cross-product from B's forward [150,170) to A's reverse [180,200): [150,200). It is rejected, as is coexistence already forbidden by the same-pool envelope-overlap rule. The disjoint-site exemption does not cover this geometry. No arbitrary new product-length threshold is necessary: disjoint outward products are geometrically longer than either individual product, whereas the exception never licenses inward overlap products.

## Symmetry, monotonicity and validation

Define intrinsic validity I(A) from all products between hits of A's concrete oligos, including self-oligo products where opposite-orientation sites exist. Only exemption 1 applies, with A's own signatures. Define pair validity P(A,B) from every cross-origin product between one oligo occurrence of A and one of B, both possible plus/minus assignments, plus existing overlap and chemistry rules. Deduplicate physical products and compare against the pair-local union of exact declared-pair signatures, followed by the strict disjoint cross-combination rule. Exemptions are evaluated only against A and B. Require I for each candidate and P for every unordered same-pool pair. Canonicalize (A,B); P(A,B)=P(B,A).

A third selected candidate or an unselected catalogue candidate must never authorize an exemption. Retain declaration ownership to construct exact signatures, but do not require every ownership derivation of the same physical product to pass independently: an exact intended product of A remains that physical product when the same oligo is also supplied by B. Sequence equality is safe for hit/chemistry reuse and physical-product deduplication, but cannot by itself transfer intended-site status at a different anchor or on a different row. I(A) and P(A,C) are fixed predicates and cannot be rescued by B; requiring all intrinsic and pair predicates makes adding candidates unable to repair an existing incompatibility. Removal cannot create a new conflict. No pool-global or catalogue-global whitelist is permitted.

Shared-oligo regression: let a 20-base plus oligo bind [0,20) and [300,320), with A's intended reverse footprint [180,200) and B's [480,500), and D=200. With no other eligible hits or chemistry conflicts, A's and B's direct products are each 200 bases; the outward 500-base product lies outside D. I(A) and I(B) pass. P(A,B) must also pass: enumerating B's shared plus oligo at [0,20) with A's reverse describes A's already-proven intended product. An every-ownership-derivation rule would incorrectly reject it. The exact pair-local signature union corrects this false conflict without any third-candidate exemption.

The independent validator must freshly enumerate I and P under the same published semantics without trusting search verdicts. Report rejected-product witnesses and allowed-secondary-product diagnostics with row, owners, orientation, coordinates, predicted length and reason. Coverage still counts declared selected candidate intervals only; tolerated longer products do not increase the objective.

Minimum hand-derived fixtures: disjoint same-row pairs pass in either order; overlapping inward products fail; an additional off-anchor hit still fails despite intended hits; cross-MSA products fail; products cannot combine different alleles; within-candidate extra products fail; exact boundary D is included; uncertain support cannot exempt; third-candidate addition cannot legalize a conflict; the shared-oligo direct-product example passes in both pair orders while shared sequence alone never exempts a different product; duplicate target occurrences stay distinct; gapped-row coordinates preserve literal product geometry.

## Legacy implementation observation

`primalscheme3/core/mismatches.py` stores MSA index and alignment position without row identity, filters away cross-MSA hits in its remove-expected paths, and uses differences between k-mer starts for product distance. Those behaviors cannot implement this profile faithfully. Keep legacy behavior isolated, as the design already requires; implement the new checker over explicit row-aware hits and immutable candidate records. Thermochemical limits, dimer checks, pool envelope constraints and the supplied-MSA scope remain unchanged.
