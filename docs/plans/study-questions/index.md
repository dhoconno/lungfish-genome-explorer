# Study Questions

Every chapter of this manual has a companion page of questions. A learner uses them to check understanding after reading a chapter. A teacher can assign them as homework, in a laboratory course, or as preparation for a discussion section.

The questions are not comprehension checks. None can be answered by searching the chapter for a sentence, because each one applies the chapter's ideas to a case the chapter does not cover, such as a new sequence, a result you have not seen, a run that went wrong, or a design decision with no single right answer.

## The three tiers

Each page is divided into three tiers. Work through the one that fits you and stop there. A tier says who it is for and what it assumes, so you can start at Beginner after a first reading and come back to Advanced later.

| Tier | Who it is for | What it assumes |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | A reader who has just finished the chapter | The chapter itself, and nothing more. Questions are short and the design project gives every step. |
| Intermediate | A reader who wants to work with real data | The Beginner tier, plus comfort with the chapter's main procedure. The design project names the goal and the route. |
| Advanced | A reader who wants to judge whether a result is fit for its purpose | Everything above, plus willingness to reach a negative conclusion. The design project states a requirement and leaves the method to you. |

## Design projects

Every tier ends with a design project in Lungfish Genome Explorer (LGE), scaled to the tier. A Beginner project is guided, uses a small record and takes under an hour. An Intermediate project is partly guided. An Advanced project is open-ended and asks you to decide what to measure.

Design projects use real public data, named by accession, that no demo project contains, so the work resembles a real question rather than a rehearsal. Each project names its records with their lengths, says how long it takes, and says what to hand in. Everything downloads and runs on a laptop.

Every design project on every page has been solved in LGE before publication, so none of them is a dead end.

## Guidance, and what to do about answers

Every question carries a collapsed **Guidance** block. It lists what a complete answer contains, names the traps, and gives the numbers where a number is the point. For a design project it also gives the measured results of a real run, so you can check your own.

Guidance is not a model answer to copy, and for an open-ended project it describes acceptable outcomes rather than one result. It is collapsed so you can think first and open it after.

Anyone reading the page can open a Guidance block. A teacher setting graded homework should change the numbers, as the "For instructors" note at the foot of each page explains. Instructors can also request the answer-key projects and the fuller marking notes, which are kept separately and are not part of this manual.

## Finding a chapter's questions

Each chapter links its own page from its `## Next` section. The nav lists the pages by part, in chapter order, and the PDF collects them as its own part at the end. In the printed version the Guidance blocks appear open, since a printed page cannot be unfolded.
