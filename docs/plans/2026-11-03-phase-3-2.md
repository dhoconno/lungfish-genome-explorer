## Outcome and budget

A lab on Preview opens every kind of result through one viewer host, with no stale or blank viewer on a switch, and the
sidebar lists every kind through one registry. A BLAST search that gets stuck at NCBI no longer fails or holds up the work.
LGE sends it again after about three minutes and uses the first copy that comes back. A search whose copies are all stuck
stays on its row as waiting and comes back on its own when NCBI finishes it, even after a quit. No test, gate or walk row
counts on how fast BLAST answers or on which tied records it names. A person can search a BLAST database of their own where the
BIO ruling allows it, and the record names that database. An OpenAI account with no credits reads as exactly that, and
not as a rate limit (GitHub issue 35). The three result rows carried from 3.1 are fixed.

## What the BLAST debugging found

The owner asked for this before the plan went out (O131). The 3.1 manager and four experts ran BLAST outside LGE on 2026-11-03
and 11-04, with one serial curl harness and the real `lungfish-cli` of builds 12045 and 11965. Start with
`~/lungfish-phase-records/blast-debug/SUMMARY.md`. The memos, `results.tsv` and every search ID are beside it. An earlier
version of this section called the holds NCBI's and planned to wait them out. The owner rejected that (O139), and the tests

### It is a known problem

Now and then a search gets stuck at NCBI. It reads `Status=WAITING` for hours, while the same request sent again comes back in
a minute or two. Biopython users have met this for years: a stalled `qblast` that a restart cured in 2015, retry loops in 2017,
searches stuck for 40 minutes in issue 2865 in 2020, and online tests that tend to time out in issue 4870 in 2024. NCBI publishes
no time and no cause, and says it favours interactive users. So LGE has to cope with it, and no test may count on it not
happening. We do not know why a search sticks, and only NCBI could say.

|---|---|
| Curl searches | 43: 32 ready at the first check (41 to 85 s), 8 ready later (102 to 137 s), 3 stuck, at 22 to 49 min when this was written |
| Real client, builds 12045 and 11965 | 7: 6 in 32 to 35 s, 1 stopped at LGE's 10 minutes and still WAITING at NCBI 50 min on, while curl sent 40 s later on the same bytes took 102 s |

| LGE's walks, 2026-10-27 to 11-02 | 7 of 20 sends stuck: rotavirus 6 of 8, all others 1 of 12. Curl on rotavirus reads has stuck 2 of 30 so far. 5 of the 7 were later READY with full results, the slowest at 26.8 h. 2 read UNKNOWN by 48 h |

| The connection | No failed contact. Status and result pages read the same from any client |

**A caution on tonight's rate.** None of the first 12 searches stuck, at a low send rate. Four of the next 38 did, at about 30 searches an hour. Our send rate may matter, so the bad-day guard below stays. The walks' holds came at about 6 an hour, so it is not the whole story.

### What LGE does (row BLAST-RESEND with BLAST-TIMEOUT-COLLECT)

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| A search is a file | `NET-CLIENT.md` section 4b sets it: saved before the POST, the request ID before any check, every state change after. No limit in the client ends a search. Only NCBI's answer or the person does |
| Send again at about three minutes | Not READY at about 3 min: POST the same bytes again. Again at about 6 min. At most three copies. Check each live copy once a minute, and the first READY wins. One constant, checked against the field log |
| A bad-day guard | Stop sending copies for an hour when several searches in a row use up their copies, and cap copies an hour. On 2026-11-02 rotavirus stuck 6 of 6 and one resend stuck too, so a copy will not always help, and a stuck NCBI should not get more load from us. ARCH sets the numbers |
| Every copy stuck | The row reads waiting at NCBI, not failed, and keeps its request IDs. Checks slow to every 5, then 15, then 30 min, and go on through NCBI's retention. Known: READY at 26.8 h, UNKNOWN by 48 h. The result comes back onto its row on its own, also after a quit, through one `lungfish-cli blast resume` per in-flight search at launch. The person can send again or stop |
| A failed contact is no news | Any status or result contact that fails is counted and tried at the next tick, as `NET-CLIENT.md` section 4b sets |
| What the record keeps | The bytes sent, every request ID tried, which copy won and when. The verdict reads the winner alone. Replay sends the same bytes |
| Counting | Every POST counts against LGE's limit, and contacts stay 11 s apart across processes. The words name LGE's limit as LGE's |

What 100% can mean (O131): **no search is lost, and that one can be promised.** Every accepted search ends in a stored result, or in NCBI's own answer shown with its ID. Time to a result is reported as a distribution and is never promised, because NCBI's queue sets it. If about 8% stick and copies draw independently, two copies leave 0.6% and three 0.05% waiting past 6 min. That is a guess until the field log shows it, and a bad day will not be independent. Each search adds a line to `<root>/blast/search-log.tsv`: ID, copies, seconds and how it ended.

### A different server or database

Recommendation, for BIO and ARCH to test. Keep NCBI `core_nt` as the default. Add a database the person controls, searched
with local `blastn`. Leave out a free server address until a lab asks for one and a real server is there to test against.

- No common server API exists. SequenceServer and EBI each have their own, EBI offers ENA and not `core_nt`, and ElasticBLAST
  is a beta command-line tool for AWS and GCP. NCBI's AWS image spoke the URL API, but its pages at
  `ncbi.github.io/blast-cloud` returned 404 on 2026-11-03.
- `blastn` is already managed, as `bioconda::blast=2.16.0` in the `full-length-mhc-genotyping` pack, and the URL API replies
  came from BLAST 2.17.0. A local `core_nt` is for a lab server and not a laptop. NCBI's metadata of 2026-10-24 gives 261 GB
  to download, 307 GB on disk, 132.3 million sequences and 92 volumes. The search speed on a Mac is not measured.
- The gains are real. Reads stay on the Mac, no queue or hourly limit applies, and a fixed database build suits a methods
  section.
- The main risk is scientific. A narrow database gives a read nothing to contradict it, which is the false Supported of the
  S1 BIO-NAOMGS-BLAST-SCOPE. A database built without tax IDs falls to the name rules that BLAST-NAME-PHRASE-LEAK describes.
- `BlastServiceEndpoints.swift` holds that no option, project file, defaults key or downloaded field can name a host, so that
  no project can send reads to a stranger. Any new choice lives in Settings, and a replayed record asks before it uses one.

the yank command. After that Preview, the issue 35 comment goes up and the issue closes.
